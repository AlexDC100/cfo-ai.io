in-code-with-722-only). The doc below is structured to support

## 3. Strip-item taxonomy (OMFP 1802 economic reality)

Per-bucket semantics for every strip item across both variants.
Reference: OMFP 1802/2014 (Ministerul Finanțelor Publice) +
Romanian chart-of-accounts class definitions (RAS).

| Canonical bucket | RAS account(s) | OMFP 1802 economic meaning | Cash-flow nature |
|---|---|---|---|
| `capitalized_own_work_memo` | 72x (`72`-prefix) | Internal labor & materials capitalized to PPE / intangibles. Revenue-side accrual that offsets COGS/personnel/opex consumption in the same period. | **Non-cash** — pure timing-shift between P&L and BS (PPE additions). |
| `inventory_variation_memo` | 711 net | Net change in finished/WIP inventory (production stocked − production consumed). RAS-specific memo; IFRS books inside COGS. **Already net-zero on clean cycles** because production wash is symmetric. | **Non-cash** when net-zero. When non-zero, represents inventory accrual (stocked > consumed = positive memo; consumed > stocked = negative). |
| `provision_reversals` | 781 | Reversal of previously-booked provisions when the obligation extinguishes (e.g. customer payment recovers a receivable provision; legal case settles below provision estimate). | **Non-cash** — credit-side reversal of the original provision accrual. |
| `provision_charges` | 6812 | Period-fresh provision additions (warranty estimates, legal accruals, restructuring reserves). | **Non-cash** — expense-side accrual of a future obligation. |
| `impairment_receivables` | 654, 6814 | Expected credit loss charges on receivables (IFRS 9 ECL equivalent). Booked when a customer's recoverability deteriorates. | **Non-cash** — write-down only; actual write-off lives in a different account. |
| `fx_gain_unrealized` / `fx_loss_unrealized` | 7651 / 6651 (sub-detail) | Period-end revaluation of FX-denominated balances (cash, AR, AP) at the closing rate. Pure mark-to-market hit; reverses on FX settlement. | **Non-cash** — settlement-date FX is realized; period-end FX is the unrealized adjustment. |
| `other_operating_income_one_off` | 758 (sub-detail) | Non-recurring operating income — legal settlements, write-back of stale payables, prior-year recoveries. | **Cash usually** but **non-recurring** — PE diligence strips because it doesn't tell you about run-rate operations. |
| `government_grants_recognized` | 740, 7584 | Government grants amortized from the deferred-income BS account over the asset's useful life. Reflects cash already received (deferred) being recognized into P&L. | **Non-cash this period** (cash was received in a prior period and parked in deferred income). |
| `gain_loss_disposal_ppe` | 7583 (gain) / 6583 (loss) | Net gain/(loss) on PPE disposals = sale proceeds − net book value. The cash piece is the proceeds; the P&L hit is just the accounting reclass between cash + P&L vs the BS PPE write-off. | **Mixed** — the gain/loss itself is non-cash (it's just the accounting wash between cash inflow and BS reduction). The cash inflow IS cash but lives outside EBITDA's operating scope. |

**Reading guide for the table.** The "Cash-flow nature" column
distinguishes items that are genuinely non-cash (provision
charges/reversals, FX revaluation, impairment, capitalized
own-work) from items that are non-recurring (one-off income,
disposal gains) from items that are accrual-only (inventory
variation). Each of these three categories deserves different
treatment in `cash` vs `strict`:

- **Non-cash** items → strip in `cash` (purpose: "what hit the
  bank"). Examples: 781, 6812, 7651/6651, 654, 72x.
- **Non-recurring** items → strip in `strict` (purpose: "run-rate
  operations"). Examples: 758 non-recurring sub-items, 7583/6583
  disposal gains.
- **Accrual-only** items → already net-zero in well-formed RAS,
  should NOT be re-stripped. Example: 711.

## 4. Strict variant — three candidate canonical formulas

### Candidate (A) — YAML is right, in-code is wrong

**Premise.** The granular sub-buckets (`one_off`, `grants`,
`disposal`) are the CORRECT analytic decomposition of 758. A
lender or PE-diligence reader cares about *which* component of
758 — was it a write-back of a stale payable (recurring), a
government grant (non-cash + non-recurring), or a disposal gain
(non-recurring + reclass)? Subtracting the lump 758 conflates
these.

**Ship sequence.**

1. Audit the canonical adapter (`country_packs/ro_romania/canonical.py`
   or wherever it lives) to carve 758 lines into the three
   sub-buckets by RAS sub-account code (7581 / 7583 / 7584).
2. Verify the carve sums to lump 758 ±0 RON per fixture.
3. F4.2-PARITY strict re-runs; expected ±0 RON across all 8
   fixtures (the sub-bucket carve sums to the lump 758, and YAML
   subtracts the same total).
4. Promote strict to HARD ±1 RON.

**LOC budget.** ~30 in the canonical adapter + smoke tests.

**Risk.** The 758 sub-account taxonomy may not be uniformly used
across fixtures (e.g. an operator may book a legal settlement to
the lump 758 with no sub-account suffix). If so, the carve mis-attributes
and shifts the strict number away from the lender-expected view.

### Candidate (B) — In-code is right, YAML is wrong

**Premise.** Lump 758 + lump 781 is the practical PE-diligence
view: "everything in 758 is non-recurring or non-cash; strip the
whole lump." Granular sub-buckets are over-engineered. The
in-code formula already produces the right number; the YAML
should match it.

**Ship sequence.**

1. Edit YAML strict to subtract lump `other_op_income.net` and
   `provision_reversals` instead of the granular sub-buckets.
2. F4.2-PARITY strict re-runs; expected ±0 RON.
3. Promote strict to HARD ±1 RON.

**LOC budget.** ~5 in the YAML.

**Risk.** Loses analytic granularity. A future analyst who wants
to know "how much of the strict strip was government grants?"
loses that visibility unless the FE consumer surface re-derives
sub-bucket totals from line_items (which is the kind of
re-derivation F4.0 was designed to eliminate).

### Candidate (C) — Both wrong, third definition needed

**Premise.** Neither lump-758 nor granular-sub-bucket is right.
The correct strict formula is reported MINUS only the *non-cash*
or *non-recurring* sub-items of 758 — NOT every 758 line. E.g.:

- Strip 7583 (disposal gains) — non-recurring.
- Strip 7584 (grants) — non-cash this period.
- Keep 7581 (legal settlements) and 7588 (other) inside the
  strict baseline because they ARE recurring in the strict sense
  (an SME with active operations regularly books small write-backs
  and settlements; that's run-rate).

**Ship sequence.**

1. Adapter carves 758 sub-buckets (same as Candidate A).
2. YAML strict subtracts ONLY the sub-buckets we decided are
   non-cash or non-recurring.
3. F4.2-PARITY strict re-runs; expected delta = `lump_758` minus
   the chosen strip subset. Compare against the operator's
   prediction.
4. Promote strict to HARD ±1 RON.

**LOC budget.** ~30 in adapter + ~5 in YAML.

**Risk.** Highest analytic ambition; lowest predictability. The
decision of "which 758 sub-buckets are recurring" is itself a
methodology judgment that varies by industry and by lender
audience.

## 5. Cash variant — three candidate canonical formulas

### Candidate (A) — YAML is right, in-code is wrong

**Premise.** Cash EBITDA SHOULD strip 711 because inventory
variation is an accrual, not a cash flow. The in-code formula
(`ebitda_cash = ebitda_operational`) is just bare-EBITDA, which
includes the 711 accrual implicitly via COGS.

**Why this premise is empirically wrong on RAS:** 711 is the
*production-variation memo*, not a "what-hit-the-bank"
inventory delta. In RAS, raw material consumption flows through
601, finished-goods stocking flows through 711, and the matching
COGS recognition flows through 711 on the credit side as
production gets consumed. On a clean production cycle, 711 nets
to ~0 because stocked = consumed over the period. **A non-zero
711 in RAS means uneven production-vs-consumption timing, not a
cash event.** Subtracting it from cash EBITDA mis-categorizes the
accrual as a non-cash item.

**This candidate is the YAML's current behavior** — and the
empirical evidence (192M delta on Agras, 83M on Frozen) is the
counterfactual: a Romanian inventory-heavy manufacturer does NOT
have −173M of "cash EBITDA" when reported EBITDA is +18M. The
−173M is the phantom 711 strip.

**Verdict (recommended):** REJECT this candidate. The YAML is
semantically wrong about what 711 represents in RAS.

### Candidate (B) — In-code is right, YAML is wrong

**Premise.** Cash EBITDA = bare operating EBITDA. The cash view
excludes 722 (capitalized own-work — non-cash) but does NOT
re-strip 711, because 711 is already netted in COGS. Provision
movements (charges + reversals), FX revaluation, and impairment
also stay out of scope because in-code cash is the simple "what
the books showed before accrual adjustments" view.

**Ship sequence.**

1. Edit YAML cash formula:
   ```yaml
   cash:
     base: reported
     subtract:
       - ?capitalized_own_work_memo  # keep — 722 IS non-cash
       # REMOVE inventory_variation_memo (already net in COGS)
       # REMOVE provision_reversals (already in reported via 781)
       # REMOVE fx_gain_unrealized (financial line, not operating)
     add: []  # remove all add-backs
   ```
2. F4.2-PARITY cash re-runs; expected ±0 RON across all 8
   fixtures.
3. Promote cash to HARD ±1 RON.

**LOC budget.** ~8 in the YAML.

**Risk.** Loses the analytic granularity of "what's the
provision-charge offset" / "what's the FX-revaluation strip."
Operators who want a stricter "what hit the bank" view than the
in-code's bare-EBITDA need a *fourth* variant — but Candidate B
preserves the F4.2-PARITY HARD invariant and keeps the YAML and
in-code byte-identical, which is the F3.16 sprint's primary goal.

### Candidate (C) — Both wrong, third definition needed

**Premise.** Cash EBITDA should strip ALL non-cash items
(722, 781, 6812 charges, 654 impairment, 7651/6651 unrealized FX)
but NOT the inventory-variation accrual. The YAML's intent is
right (strip non-cash); the YAML's implementation has the
711-double-strip bug.

**Ship sequence.**

1. Edit YAML cash formula:
   ```yaml
   cash:
     base: reported
     subtract:
       - ?capitalized_own_work_memo
       # REMOVE inventory_variation_memo
       - ?provision_reversals
       - ?fx_gain_unrealized
     add:
       - ?provision_charges
       - ?fx_loss_unrealized
       - ?impairment_receivables
   ```
2. Edit in-code `ebitda_cash` to subtract the same set:
   ```python
   ebitda_cash = (ebitda + capitalized
                  - provision_reversals_781
                  + provision_charges_6812
                  + impairment_receivables_654
                  - fx_gain_unrealized_7651
                  + fx_loss_unrealized_6651)
   ```
3. F4.2-PARITY cash re-runs; expected ±0 RON.
4. Promote cash to HARD ±1 RON.

**LOC budget.** ~10 in YAML + ~25 in in-code.

**Risk.** Most analytically ambitious. The "non-cash" definition
expands the in-code formula and requires the adapter to populate
all six bucket totals correctly across the 8 fixtures.

## 6. Coupling consideration

**Are the strict and cash decisions coupled?** Three possibilities:

1. **Coupled symmetric** — both pick Candidate B (in-code is
   right; flatten YAML to match). Lowest-LOC ship; loses
   granularity for both. F4.2-PARITY 8/8 GREEN on all 4 variants.
2. **Coupled asymmetric** — strict picks Candidate A or C
   (sub-bucket carve makes strict more analytically useful) +
   cash picks Candidate B (the simpler in-code path is already
   correct semantically). Phase 3 ships YAML cash edit + adapter
   sub-bucket carve. F4.2-PARITY 8/8 GREEN on all 4.
3. **Independent** — strict picks Candidate C (mixed
   sub-bucket strip) + cash picks Candidate C (non-cash strip
   excluding 711). Two adapter changes + two YAML edits + two
   in-code edits. Highest LOC, highest analytic value.

**Recommended default for Phase 2 operator review:** Option 2
(coupled asymmetric). Reasoning: cash's primary value is being a
clean "what hit the bank" headline (Candidate B is simplest);
strict's primary value is supporting PE diligence with granular
visibility (Candidate A's sub-bucket carve is highest-value).
But the operator may have different judgment based on the
external-reference survey (§7).
