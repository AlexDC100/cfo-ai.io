# THE CAPSULE — ZERO-SPEND COVERAGE, MEASURED END TO END

> **K3 reports 51.4% (37/72).** That is a true statement about a
> function: `resolveTier0(q, index)`, called in jsdom, against a fixture
> index.
>
> **Driven through the real surface, against a real Romanian trial
> balance, the figure is 34.7% (25/72).**
>
> The gap is 12 questions. This page shows exactly which twelve, and why
> — and the answer is not a defect in the surface. It is that K3's
> denominator is measured against a dataset richer than the one a real
> workspace with one uploaded period actually has.
>
> Driver `scripts/capsule_demo.mjs coverage` · raw `coverage-e2e.json` ·
> tables regenerated by `tools/coverage_report.py` so no number here is
> retyped · code state `MEASURED_TREE.md` · 2026-08-30.

---

## HOW IT WAS MEASURED

All 72 questions of `frontend/lib/capsuleAskCorpus.ts` — loaded by
transpiling that file and importing it, not re-typed and not
regex-scraped, so the denominator cannot drift from the file it quotes.

For each question: **a fresh browser context**, the real dashboard on a
real period, ⌘K, type, Enter, wait for a turn, read the DOM. The two
model seams K10 names are recorded from the browser's own request stream
and aborted, so the count is exact and nothing is billed.

One page load per question is not politeness. `capsuleAskGuard` is module
state — a 1.5 s minimum gap and 6 asks per rolling minute — so a single
long-lived page would have had question 7 onwards REFUSED, spending
nothing, and scoring as coverage. The percentage would then have risen
as a function of how fast the driver typed. That is canary **C-GUARD**.

**Canary C-SPEND**: at least one question must reach a model seam.
47 did. A run where nothing ever spent would mean the counter was not
wired, and this page would refuse to report a number.

## WHERE THE 12-QUESTION GAP IS

The Tier-0 preview's own verdict, read from the `data-refused` attribute
`CapsuleTier0Preview` sets itself:

| Verdict | n |
|---|---:|
| **resolved** — a figure, with provenance | **24** |
| **refused** — Tier 0 engaged and declined | **11** |
| absent — Tier 0 never offered anything | 37 |

`24 + 11 = 35`. **K3 reports exactly 35 Tier-0 hits.**

So the resolver's *reach* is identical in both measurements: the same 35
questions engage it. The difference is entirely in whether the data
behind it can answer them. K3's fixture index can. A real workspace with
**one** uploaded period cannot, and says so.

The eleven, verbatim:

| Question | Why it refuses on one period |
|---|---|
| how did revenue change vs last month | needs ≥2 periods |
| show me the revenue trend over time | needs ≥3 periods |
| care e marja netă față de luna trecută | needs ≥2 periods |
| what changed vs last month | needs ≥2 periods |
| compare December and November revenue | needs ≥2 periods |
| what changed vs FY 2024 | needs ≥2 periods |
| revenue vs FY 2024 | needs ≥2 periods |
| what findings fired this month | needs the findings rows |
| cine sunt cei mai mari clienți | *"the total is on file … but the split behind it is not — a trial balance carries the balance, not the counterparty"* |
| cash conversion cycle by product line | same refusal — no per-product split in a trial balance |
| ce înseamnă datorie netă | a definition, not a figure |

**Seven of the eleven are period comparisons.** They are refusing
correctly. A one-period workspace genuinely cannot compare two months,
and Tier 0 saying so is the product working — F1's law, *"ABSENT is not
ZERO"*, applied to a question instead of a fact.

### What that means for K3's headline

K3's failure message says *"there are ZERO real coverage gaps"*. On K3's
fixture index that is true. On a real workspace holding one period it is
also true — and the achievable ceiling is **25, not 37**, because eleven
of the thirty-five are unanswerable until a second period is uploaded.

Neither figure is wrong. They answer different questions:

* **51.4%** — *of the corpus, how much CAN this tier answer when the data
  is there?*
* **34.7%** — *of the corpus, how much DOES a user with one period get
  for free today?*

The second is the one a reader of a delivery pack thinks they are being
told. **This lane did not move K3's floor, its corpus, or its
predicate.** It measured the other number and is publishing both.

## BY WHERE THE QUESTION CAME FROM

The corpus carries its own provenance column, so the figure can be split
by what kind of question it is:

| Source | n | free | routed | spent | % free |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| `brief` — the metric classes, EN + RO | 19 | **15** | 0 | 4 | **78.9%** |
| `answer_fixture` — retrieval branches | 12 | 5 | 1 | 6 | 50.0% |
| `router_fixture` — the ask lane | 14 | 3 | 0 | 11 | 21.4% |
| `production_log` — real user questions | 3 | 0 | 0 | 3 | 0% |
| `suggestion` — the product's own chips | 24 | 1 | 0 | 23 | 4.2% |

This is the shape of the whole result in one table.

**The lookup questions are nearly free.** Of the 19 plain metric
questions a person types when they want a number — *"total assets"*,
*"cifra de afaceri"*, *"how much cash do we have"*, *"e echilibrat
bilanțul?"* — **15 cost nothing** and paint a provenanced figure in
34.7 ms.

**The suggestion chips are almost entirely paid, and that is by design.**
24 of the 72 corpus entries are the chips the product itself offers, and
they exist to *start a conversation*: *"Why did inventory get flagged
this month?"*, *"Is the drift something to worry about?"*. Tier 0
answering those would mean inventing an opinion. 23 of 24 reach the
model, correctly.

**All three real production-log questions are paid.** All three are
interpretation requests. A tier that answers lookups cannot help with
*"Explain our current cash flow position in plain language for the
management team."* — and should not try.

So the honest one-line reading of 34.7% is: **the free tier answers
79% of the lookups and 4% of the judgement prompts**, and the corpus is
one-third judgement prompts by construction.

## THE INSTRUMENT ERRORS THIS RUN CAUGHT IN ITSELF

Both were found by re-running anomalies individually instead of
accepting the census. Both would have been published as product defects.

**1. A refusal counted as a resolution.** The first predicate asked *"does
the Tier-0 preview contain a digit?"* Two questions came back "resolved
in the preview, then billed on Enter" — a spend-boundary leak. Re-run on
their own, both showed no fact card at all. Their previews read:

> *"The total is on file for **Dec 2025**, but the split behind it is not
> — a trial balance carries the balance, not the counterparty."*

A refusal that names the period it is refusing about. `2025` is a digit.
The predicate now reads `data-refused`, the attribute the component sets
itself. **The reported leak did not exist**; the corrected run shows 0.

**2. Enter racing the surface.** One question, *"how much do we owe
suppliers"*, was reported as free-but-unanswered — no turn created at
all — while answering free every time it was asked on its own. The driver
now waits for `capsule-turn`, retries once, and reports a still-missing
turn as an **instrument error excluded from the denominator**, never as a
product miss. One retry fired in the final run; zero errors remain.

The first version of this page would have reported one product defect
that was a driver flake and one that was a bad predicate. Neither
survived being checked.

## WHAT THIS PAGE DOES NOT CLAIM

* **It is one dataset.** One real company, one period. A workspace with
  five periods would move the eleven refusals — probably most of them —
  into the free column. Nothing here says 34.7% is the number for every
  workspace; it says it is the number for *this* one, which is also the
  shape of the most common real workspace: one upload.
* **It is one run.** 72 questions, one page load each, no repeats. A
  question near a boundary could land differently on another run; the
  raw per-question rows are in `coverage-e2e.json` so any of them can be
  re-checked individually.
* **Aborting the seams is not free of consequence.** A question is
  counted as having spent when the REQUEST was made. Whether the model
  would have answered well is a different question this page does not
  ask.
